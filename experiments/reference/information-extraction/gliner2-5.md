## Description

GLiNER2.5 keeps GLiNER2's prompt and encoder and replaces the span grid. For
each request, called a query, the model scores every token boundary as a
possible start, every token boundary as a possible end, and every token as
being inside a span of that query. A proposal stage keeps the best starts
and, for each, the best ends that follow it, then the same backwards from the
best ends, and the union, cut to a budget, is the candidate set. Each
candidate pair is then rescored with the inside evidence between its ends and
its length. Because a start may pair with any later end, a span can open at
the first token and close at the last, and the work stays linear in the text
length for a fixed schema and budget.

- **Relations are typed pairs of mentions.** A relation group names a head
  role and a tail role, and the schema says which entity types may fill each.
  For every relation type the best head-typed and tail-typed mentions are
  paired and scored together with the relation's query vector.
- **Attributes classify a span.** An attribute such as sentiment or negation
  is a classification whose query is scored on an already extracted span, so a
  mention carries its own labels.
- **Records lose the count head.** A record is formed around an anchor field;
  every anchor candidate seeds one record and the other fields are assigned to
  a record or declared absent.
- **Decoding is constrained.** Classification tasks may declare rules between
  labels, relations declare their endpoint types and multiplicity, and the
  decoder returns only assignments that satisfy them; the `type-safe-encoder`
  entry describes it.

## What the paper adds

There is no paper. The release note states three claims: a span may have any
length within the window, inference stays linear in the length, and outputs
satisfy the declared schema. On the vendor's zero-shot suite the multilingual
model scores level with GLiNER2 overall and far above it on natural language
inference, and its training data was generated by the vendor's own data agent
rather than drawn from public corpora.

## Why it matters here

GLiNER2.5 is the current shape of the head this project would train, and the
first whose candidate count does not grow with a width limit: a Slovenian
document of 4,000 words is scored on 192 candidates per query instead of a
grid of tens of thousands. Its relation and attribute heads cover what the
NER benchmarks here do not. The cost is that none of its parts has a paper
and the recipe exists only as code, so what a Slovenian backbone would need
of it is read from the implementation.

## Facts

- **Tasks**: named entity recognition, classification, record extraction, typed relation extraction, span attributes, and any combination in one pass
- **Prompt tokens**: GLiNER2's, plus `[R]` before the head and tail roles of a relation group
- **Span candidates**: for each query, the top $k$ starts by start score paired with the 12 best ends after each, the same from the top $k$ ends backwards, duplicates removed, cut to 192; $k$ is 24, raised with the length to at most 128
- **Longest span**: the text; no width limit
- **Scoring**: start, end and inside scores as scaled dot products between the query vector and boundary or token vectors; a candidate's logit is the pair's endpoint compatibility plus its start and end scores plus the mean inside score between them plus length and content terms
- **Decoding**: candidates with sigmoid above 0.5, or filled up to a predicted count, under a flat overlap policy; relations over the 32 best head and tail mentions per type, at most 64 pairs; classification and relations then pass through the constrained decoder
- **Backbone**: DeBERTa-v3-base for the base model, mDeBERTa-v3-base for the multilingual one, a 74M small model
- **Context length**: 4,096 words
- **Training data**: generated by the vendor's data agent; size not published
- **Boundary head**: boundary vectors of width 128 from the left and right token vectors, two local attention blocks with a window of 128 and one gated feed-forward block; the pair scorer has width 128 with 8 compatibility heads and rotary endpoint positions
- **Training**: an asymmetric focal loss over start and end scores, binary cross-entropy over candidate pairs with 20 hard negatives per positive, a listwise loss that ranks gold pairs first, a Poisson loss on the count head, binary cross-entropy on labels and relations, weighted and summed
- **Sizes**: small 74M, base 0.2B, multilingual 0.3B parameters

- **Example**: `experiments/reference/information-extraction/examples.py`
- **Paper vs code**: not checked against project code. The release note says the model predicts start and end scores and pairs them without a width limit; the code also scores every token as inside, keeps a running sum of those scores so any interval's inside evidence costs one subtraction, and adds length features and a pooled content vector to each candidate. ## Difference from predecessor

- **Line**: 4 of GLiNER2's Algorithm 1, the span grid, against lines 2 to 8 of Algorithm 1 here
- **Predecessor**: every span of at most 8 words is built from its two end vectors and scored against each field vector
- **Here**: boundary vectors are scored as starts and ends per query, the best are paired sparsely at any distance, and each pair is rescored with the inside evidence between its ends
- **Change**: a sparse boundary proposal replaces the width-limited span grid; relations, attributes and constrained decoding are added.
- **Effect**: A span may be as long as the text, the candidate count is a fixed budget rather than length times width, and relations and label rules enter the output.

## Algorithm

```algorithm
\caption{GLiNER2.5 inference: boundary scores, sparse pairing and rescoring, one query}
\Require words $w_1, \dots, w_N$, a schema prompt as GLiNER2 with $[\mathrm{R}]$ roles, encoder $E$, budgets $k_0 = 24$, $\alpha = 0.08$, $k_{max} = 128$, ends per start $e = 12$, candidate budget $C = 192$, threshold $\tau = 0.5$
\Ensure scored spans per query, handed to the constrained decoder
\State $H \gets E(x)$ with $x$ as GLiNER2's Algorithm 1, line 1; $u \gets$ the vector at the query's field marker; $h_i \gets$ the vector at the first subword of $w_i$
\Changed \State $b_i \gets \mathrm{LN}(W_o [\, W_l h_{i} \oplus W_r h_{i+1} \,])$ for the $N + 1$ boundaries, with learned vectors before $w_1$ and after $w_N$; two attention blocks over a window of 128 boundaries and one gated feed-forward block refine $b$
\Changed \State $a_i \gets \langle W_s b_i, W_s' u \rangle / \sqrt{d}$, $e_i \gets \langle W_e b_i, W_e' u \rangle / \sqrt{d}$, $n_i \gets \langle W_n h_i, W_n' u \rangle / \sqrt{d}$: start, end and inside scores
\Changed \State $k \gets \min(k_{max}, \lceil \max(k_0, \lceil \alpha N \rceil) / 8 \rceil \cdot 8)$; $S \gets$ the $k$ boundaries with the largest $a_i$; $T \gets$ the $k$ with the largest $e_i$
\Changed \State $P \gets \{(i, j) : i \in S, j \text{ among the } e \text{ ends after } i \text{ with the largest } \langle g(u) \odot W_p b_i, W_q b_j \rangle + e_j \} \cup \{(i, j) : j \in T, i \text{ among the } e \text{ starts before } j \text{ likewise}\}$
\Changed \State $C_q \gets$ the $C$ pairs of $P$ with the largest proposal score, duplicates removed
\Changed \ForAll{$(i, j) \in C_q$}
  \Changed \State $\ell_{ij} \gets \mathrm{compat}(b_i, b_j, u) + a_i + e_j + \mathrm{prior}_{ij} + \omega(u) \cdot \sum_{i \le t < j} n_t / \sqrt{j - i} + \lambda(u) \cdot \mathrm{len}(i, j) + \mathrm{content}(i, j, u)$
\EndFor
\Changed \State $R_q \gets \{(i, j) : \sigma(\ell_{ij}) \ge \tau\}$, extended to the count head's predicted number when adaptive, overlaps resolved by score
\State relations: for each relation type, the 32 best head-typed and 32 best tail-typed mentions of $R$, at most 64 pairs, scored by a bilinear layer over the two mention vectors and the relation's query
\State labels: $\sigma(\mathrm{MLP}_l(u_{g,k}))$ per label marker; attributes: the attribute query scored on each extracted span of the types it applies to
\Return every candidate with its score, for the constrained decoder
```

- **1**: The prompt and encoder are GLiNER2's. Each field marker, including the head and tail roles of a relation, is one query with its own vector.
- **2**: A boundary sits between two words. Its vector joins the word on its left and the word on its right, so the start of a span is described by what precedes it and what it begins with. Learned vectors stand in for the missing neighbour at the two ends of the text. Local attention lets boundaries see 128 neighbours and a feed-forward block refines them.
- **3**: Three scores per query, each a scaled dot product with the query vector: how much a boundary looks like a start, how much like an end, and how much a token looks like part of the span. The inside scores are summed into a running total so any interval's inside evidence is one subtraction.
- **4**: The budget grows with the length, from 24 boundaries to at most 128, rounded up to a multiple of 8 so batches share shapes. The top starts and the top ends are chosen by their scores alone.
- **5**: Each chosen start is paired with the 12 ends after it that fit it best, by a gated dot product between the two boundary vectors plus the end score; each chosen end is paired with the 12 starts before it likewise. No term depends on the distance, which is what removes the width limit. At most $2 \cdot k \cdot e$ pairs are ever materialised.
- **6**: The two directions are merged, duplicates collapsed, and the 192 best pairs kept. This is the candidate set; nothing outside it can be returned.
- **7** and **8**: Each candidate is rescored with everything known about it: the endpoint compatibility through 8 heads, its start and end scores, the proposal score as a prior, the mean inside score of the tokens between its ends, three length features, and a pooled vector of its content. The query decides how much the inside and length terms weigh.
- **9**: A candidate is an entity if its sigmoid clears the threshold. With adaptive decoding, a count head's Poisson rate fills the list up to the predicted number. The flat policy drops a candidate that overlaps a better one.
- **10**: Relations reuse the extracted mentions. The schema says which types may be a head and which a tail, so only typed pairs are formed, and a cap keeps the pair count fixed. A bilinear scorer reads both mention vectors and the relation's query.
- **11**: Labels are classified from their markers as in GLiNER2. An attribute is a classification run on a span: the attribute's query is scored on each extracted span of the types it applies to.
- **12**: Nothing is final here. Candidates, relations and labels go to the constrained decoder, which decides what the schema allows.

## Limits

### No paper

The method, its losses and its training data are described in a release
note and a model card. The claims are the vendor's own benchmarks. An
experiment here would be the first independent measurement.

### A span outside the budget is lost

If a gold span's start is not among the $k$ best starts, or its end not among
the 12 best ends for that start or the reverse, it is never scored. Recall on
long or unusual spans depends on the proposer, and the proposer is trained
with gold pairs injected, which inference lacks.

### The budget is per query, the pool per text

The shared candidate pool is built once from the start and end scores of all
queries, so a query with few strong boundaries competes with the others for
the same 192 slots.

### Thresholds and budgets are tuned to the vendor's data

Every number above, from the window of 128 to the 20 hard negatives, is a
configuration value chosen on undisclosed training data. Which of them a
Slovenian retraining must keep is unknown.
